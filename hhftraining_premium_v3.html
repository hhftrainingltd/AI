The challenge behind the technology

Having AI tools is one thing.
Knowing how to use them is another.

Your staff may already be experimenting with AI. The harder questions are practical: which tasks are suitable, what information is safe to share, how outputs should be checked, and who remains responsible.

HHF connects AI capability with teaching, assessment, quality assurance and workplace practice. The aim is a more capable team with clearer boundaries and useful ways of working.

Before we get started

Ask us a question

Who is HHF’s AI training designed for?

Training supports education teams, businesses, public services, healthcare and care organisations, individual professionals and community groups. Choose a sector pathway or ask us to shape the content around your staff roles and priorities.

Is the training suitable for complete beginners?

Yes. Training can start with what generative AI does, where it can help and where it can fail. Practical activities can be adjusted to staff confidence, with more focused workflows for experienced participants.

Can you deliver AI workshops online or at our workplace?

HHF offers in-house workshops, live online training and bespoke consultancy. Discuss your team’s location, accessibility needs, preferred format and availability so the delivery arrangements can be agreed before booking.

How much does AI training cost?

Pricing depends on the programme, participant group, delivery format and preparation required. Request a tailored proposal that sets out the scope, delivery arrangements, resources and agreed support. Prices are confirmed before booking.

Does training cover tools such as ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot?

The focus is transferable AI literacy, prompting, checking and practical workflows. Discuss the tools your organisation already approves, including ChatGPT or Microsoft Copilot, so suitable exercises and any account requirements can be agreed in advance.

How do you handle confidential or personal information in training?

Training exercises should use fictional, public or suitably anonymised material. Staff learn to check tool approval, understand what information must stay out of a prompt and review outputs. Organisational policies and current data protection requirements remain authoritative.

How do you protect assessment integrity when teaching AI?

Education pathways address permitted use, learner ownership, evidence checking and recorded assessor judgement. Requirements depend on the qualification and awarding organisation. Teams should apply the rules relevant to their assessments rather than assuming one policy covers every programme.

Can you help with AI policy, quality assurance or centre approval?

Yes. Support includes AI readiness and policy work, assessment design, IQA, standardisation, evidence tracking and training centre development. HHF helps centres prepare their systems and evidence. Approval decisions remain with the relevant awarding organisation.

What should we prepare before a consultation?

Bring your staff roles, current confidence, approved tools and a few examples of tasks you want to improve. Share the type of work rather than confidential documents. This helps identify the right starting point, likely training needs and any governance questions.
